    How a thing gets here

    Four stages between a campaign page and our shelf. Every product in the catalogue is labelled with the one it is in, so you always know what you are buying and when it lands.

    1. Stage 01

      Spotted

      We find it on a campaign page or a trade floor and put it on the watch list. Nothing is for sale yet — leave your email and we will tell you the day the campaign opens, not a week later.

    2. Stage 02

      Backed

      We put our own money into the campaign and take delivery of the first samples. Plenty of things stop here and you never see them. The ones that continue get a ship month you can hold us to.

    3. Stage 03

      Shipping

      The production run landed. Your order goes out from our shelf in a day or two, not from a factory nine time zones away with a tracking number that sits still for a month.

    4. Stage 04

      In stock

      It earned a permanent spot in the catalogue. Reorderable, supported, and stocked with the spares that wear out first — gaskets, bits, batteries, the parts nobody else bothers to carry.

    We buy the first run so you are not the test batch

    First production runs are where the problems live: the hinge that loosens, the app that never ships, the battery that lasts a third of what the page promised. We buy them, use them for a month, and only list what holds up. If something we sold goes bad later, we pull it and say why.

    Every product ships with its drawing

    A lot of what we sell does not exist as a photograph yet — it is still in tooling. So we draw it: a measured line diagram with the parts called out, next to a spec table and a note on why we stock it. You get the dimensions and the port count before you buy, not a lifestyle shot on a kitchen counter.

    The spares outlive the product

    The reason good gear gets thrown out is a $4 part nobody sells. We keep the gaskets, bits, filters and batteries on the shelf for everything we list, and we keep them there after the product stops being new. Filter prices are printed on the box as a yearly cost, never a subscription.
